# RTX 5070: convolution parallelism and the accelerated gap

The next target is convolution backward execution, not a longer tuning budget.
With the final engine and search policy, all eligible classes are visited.
Thirteen weight-gradient dispatches still launch fewer workgroups than the
GPU has SMs. Accelerated PyTorch also uses TF32 tensor-core kernels where
Meganeura retains scalar f32 derivatives. No production kernel or submitted
paper artifact changed in this investigation.

## Where the time goes

Nsight Systems 2026.4.1, three-call means in milliseconds:

| Training quantity | Native strict | Torch strict | Native accelerated | Torch accelerated |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Host sample, including input preparation | 34.480 | 14.894 | 35.391 | 7.753 |
| Host command recording | 4.614 | not separately marked | 5.102 | not separately marked |
| Grouped Vulkan GPU span | 29.314 | n/a | 29.745 | n/a |
| Sum of CUDA kernel intervals | n/a | 14.584 | n/a | 7.550 |

Native GPU execution alone is longer than the entire reference host sample.
The encoding cost matters but cannot explain most of the difference. Grouped
spans include gaps, CUDA intervals may overlap, and host ranges are elapsed
time. None of their differences measures busy CPU time or removable barriers.

Native strict inference's grouped GPU span is 4.584 ms, versus 6.624 ms of
CUDA kernel intervals. In accelerated mode those become 4.879 and 2.464 ms.
This is principally a backward/acceleration problem, not uniformly slow Vulkan
execution. These instrumented samples do not replace the submitted measurements.

The separate strict dispatch profile has 512 dispatches and 273 barrier groups.
Normal training is 31.734 ms; profiled wall time is 35.985 ms (1.13x), with
32.768 ms of GPU pass intervals. Sums of dispatch medians give:

| Family | Dispatches | ms | Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Convolution weight gradients | 53 | 16.001 | 48.9% |
| Convolution input gradients | 52 | 7.612 | 23.2% |
| Weight gradients with fewer than 48 workgroups | 13 | 11.389 | 34.8% |

The last row is a subset, not additional time. The stem is still the largest
single dispatch: 2.769 ms, `[5,2,1]` workgroups, output `[64,147]`, reduction
length 50,176. It now uses the selected constant-indexed K=32 implementation.
The earlier two-second-search diagnostic retained the generic K=16 pipeline;
that study's timings are not a controlled before/after comparison.

Strict CUDA kernel names attribute 2.872 ms/call to weight-gradient kernels and
3.173 ms to input-gradient kernels. Name matching is not full operator
attribution: conversions, reductions and work shared with other operators
must not be omitted from a claimed operator speedup.

## What Nsight Graphics adds

Graphics 2026.3.1 retains the grouped schedule, three submissions, a 200 ms
maximum and a 100 MB actual PMA buffer. No event-loss warning is reported.
The trace contains a complete 29.672 ms GPU-projected training sample:

| Peak-normalized metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| SM throughput | 32.09% |
| Device-memory throughput | 8.29% |
| Active compute warps | 38.23% |
| L1TEX-dependency stalled warps | 12.37% |
| Short-scoreboard stalled warps | 5.03% |
| Workgroup-barrier stalled warps | 2.60% |

The stem shader uses 40 registers/thread and 8 KiB shared memory/workgroup.
Nsight reports a theoretical limit of 48 resident warps/SM for this shader,
yet the entire launch contains only ten 256-thread workgroups. Lowering register
pressure alone cannot fill otherwise idle SMs.

Source-correlated samples concentrate on input staging and dependencies:
5,649 of the stem's 14,502 samples report long-scoreboard stalls at the
input-bounds branch, and 1,579 report workgroup-barrier stalls at the shared-B
load. Stalls can be attributed to a consumer or branch shadow; they do not
mean the bounds check itself costs 5,649 instructions. The generic small dW
shader's dominant samples likewise fall on staging, shared loads and their
consumers. This supports testing more independent work and better staging.

The stem contributes only 2.76% of whole-trace shader samples despite being
the largest dispatch in the separate timestamp profile. Shader samples weight
resident warps, not elapsed dispatch time; do not prioritize only by PC share.
The shader CSV's early sample/register/shared-memory columns agree with the UI.
Later headers are misaligned in this tool version and are not consumed.

Low memory throughput does not exclude memory-latency stalls. The 2.60% warp
counter is not Vulkan resource-barrier overhead. See NVIDIA's
[shader-profiler semantics](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-graphics/UserGuide/shader-profiler.html)
and [GPU Trace reference](https://docs.nvidia.com/nsight-graphics/UserGuide/gpu-trace-ui.html).

## Search coverage and precision are separate constraints

Strict training visits 71/71 classes in 26.116 s, making 343 comparisons.
Accelerated training visits 59/59 in 23.660 s. It has fewer eligible classes
because selected f16 cooperative paths are outside the current scratch search;
all eligible classes is not all conceivable legal implementations.

Ten strict comparisons fail at **variant zero**, the existing baseline:
five each for dW GEMMs `[64,576,12544]` and `[256,512,3136]`. The challengers
are not executed. These four live dispatches total 3.520 ms in the strict
profile. Increasing the time budget cannot unlock them. The model comparison
passes; the separate synthetic f64/cross-variant screen rejects these controls.
Do not label this evidence that every alternative was tried and lost, or
weaken the screen to admit faster candidates. First localize the numerical
failure and test accurate accumulation/decomposition independently.

In the accelerated CUDA trace, TF32 tensor-op/xmma-labelled kernels account for
4.416 ms/call. Both dgrad and wgrad tensor-core implementations are present.
Native has 25 cooperative dispatches in training but no cooperative convolution
derivatives; those retain f32 operands to preserve gradient range. TF32 retains
FP32's exponent range, unlike direct f16 conversion
([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/accelerating-ai-training-with-tf32-tensor-cores/)).
These are distinct implementation/representation choices, not proof that Vulkan
cannot access the relevant hardware. Do not force raw f16 backward arithmetic.

## Next bounded experiments

1. Add smaller/rectangular output tiles to measured convolution choices.
   They can expose more workgroups without a second pass or a changed reduction
   order. Test full outputs and whole-step gains, not just one hot kernel.
2. Revisit split reductions with numerically stable partial accumulation.
   Charge scratch, the final reduction and synchronization. Earlier rejected
   split-K controls remain rejected; profile evidence is not qualification.
3. Extend measured selection to legal forward cooperative/scalar alternatives,
   and investigate exponent-preserving tensor-core representations for backward.
   Keep capability and precision checks separate from performance selection.

No kernel speedup is claimed here. No model/card-specific threshold, new
regression executable, runtime arithmetic change or collection-protocol change
was added.
